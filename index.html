Investment Interpretation

The capital-flow signal favors companies with pricing power at choke points: NVIDIA in accelerated compute, Broadcom and Marvell in custom silicon and networking, Micron in HBM and server memory, TSMC/ASML/AMAT/KLAC in capacity formation, Credo/Astera in rack connectivity, and Coherent/Lumentum in optical interconnects. The June 2026 SIA/Deloitte rack study strengthens the silicon-content thesis by framing semiconductors as the overwhelming value share inside AI data server racks. The June 10 supply-chain pass keeps one more rule: structural winners do not become automatic buys when the market is repricing the same bottleneck too aggressively.

AI Data Center Industry Chain Trend

The June 10 supply-chain pass says the bottleneck is migrating from single accelerators into the full AI factory stack. The best long-term stocks are not simply the fastest growers; they are the companies whose economics sit at scarce layers that hyperscalers cannot skip.

Bottleneck Migration

Chip -> Rack -> Site

NVIDIA's Vera Rubin ramp, GB300 rack evidence, and Schneider's grid-to-chip framing all point to the same direction: deployment speed now depends on rack power, cooling, networking, and serviceability as much as accelerator supply.

Compute + Custom ASICs

  • NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra and Vera Rubin roadmaps drive rack-scale upgrades and keep the platform layer scarce.
  • Broadcom's June 3 8-K confirms custom AI accelerators and AI networking as a revenue engine, with AI semiconductor revenue expected above $16.0B in fiscal Q3.
  • AMD, Google TPU, AWS Trainium, Meta ASIC, and hyperscaler co-design programs expand custom silicon diversity, but foundry allocation, HBM, and software maturity still decide deployment speed.
  • Trend: GPUs remain the platform core; ASICs gain share in stable inference workloads where hyperscalers can optimize total cost per token.

HBM + Server Memory

  • TrendForce's June 10 press-center note says Vera CPU memory configuration changes reflect insufficient LPDRAM allocation rather than a reduction in NVIDIA memory demand.
  • TrendForce's HBM work says HBM wafer input among the top three suppliers can reach about 30% of total DRAM wafer input by the end of 2027.
  • Kioxia's investor-day plan points to larger NAND capex and data center/enterprise storage as the growth engine.
  • Trend: Micron has powerful leverage to HBM and server DRAM, but cyclicality and fast price moves require staged entries.

Foundry + Packaging + Tools

  • SEMI's Q2 2026 300mm outlook projects record US$142 billion front-end equipment spending in 2026.
  • Advanced-node wafers, HBM-related memory capacity, CoWoS-style packaging, test, inspection, and metrology form the hidden capacity layer behind each AI rack.
  • Trend: KLA and Applied Materials remain quality compounders, but the June 5 drawdown shows even best-in-class tools can fail an entry-timing gate.

Connectivity + Optics

  • 800G to 1.6T transitions raise bandwidth, DSP, retimer, AEC, laser, and switch-silicon content.
  • TrendForce says combined monthly EML and CW-DFB LD capacity can double to about 50.7 million units in 2026 as NVIDIA, Google, and Meta lock supply.
  • SIA/Deloitte's AI rack study frames semiconductors as more than 95% of AI server-rack value, supporting the long-run connectivity-content thesis.
  • Credo's Q4 call added a sharper roadmap signal: AECs, optical DSPs, silicon-photonics PICs, ZeroFlap Optics, and retimers all become AI rack bottleneck exposure.
  • Trend: connectivity content per accelerator rises, but smaller optical and cable names deserve higher concentration and valuation penalties.

Power + Cooling

  • Schneider Electric frames AI factory infrastructure as an integrated power, liquid-cooling, and digital platform from grid to chip and chip to chiller.
  • ServeTheHome's GB300 rack coverage shows rack-scale systems tied to CDUs, liquid cooling, InfiniBand, and high-density facilities.
  • Power shelves, PSUs, backup power, substations, CDUs, cold plates, and facility water loops are now deployment constraints, not secondary accessories.
  • Trend: the current dashboard keeps the buy screen focused on US-listed semiconductor technology stocks, but it scores power efficiency and rack deployment risk more strictly.
